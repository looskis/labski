# labski

labski publishes reproducible experiments on local AI hardware. Each research scope keeps its own code, hardware assumptions and results.

| Research scope | First platform | What you can reproduce |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Inference recipes](inference-recipes/) | Four NVIDIA DGX Sparks | Serve open-weight models and measure how checkpoint precision, speculative decoding and the number of Sparks affect inference. |

## Measured on DGX Spark

- **DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 on four Sparks.** Direct RoCE decode raised one-client throughput from 37.63 to 43.61 tokens/s and four-client throughput from 61.79 to 72.21. All 60 paired visible outputs matched, including 20/28 correct short answers and eight exact lookups. [Reproduce both transports](inference-recipes/results/deepseek-v41-flash-1017-2026-11-20/)
- **MiMo-V2.6-Pro MOPD on four Sparks.** The 1.02T model served one client at 5.85 tok/s and four at 11.74 aggregate. Only the head needed the 419 GB GGUF. [Placement, quality and GPU energy](inference-recipes/results/mimo-v26-pro-mopd-rpc-2026-11-20/)
- **MiMo-V2.6-Flash MOPD.** One Spark served Q2_K_S at 20.23 / 38.72 output tok/s for one / four clients. The Q8_0 MTP2 sidecar delivered 22.37 / 43.32. One draft token was within 0.3% of two at both client counts, while three slowed generation. On two Sparks both conversions scored 81/112 constrained answers, with seven gains and seven losses. Q2_K_S led MXFP4 at four clients, 38.82 vs 28.00 tok/s. [Quality and GPU energy](inference-recipes/results/mimo-v26-flash-mopd-2026-11-20/)
- **Ling 3.1 Flash 1019 on one Spark.** Use two native draft tokens for one client (22.67 tok/s) and one draft token for four (46.65 aggregate). At four clients, four draft tokens dropped below ordinary decoding. All three depths scored 15/28 constrained letters. [ARM64 recipe, timing and output checks](inference-recipes/results/ling31-flash-1019-exl3-2026-11-20/)
- **MiniMax M3.1 Flash on one Spark.** The ARM64 Maya build served the 93 GB XS release at 30.25 aggregate tok/s for four clients. Capping GPU-resident experts lowered that to 23.93. Both runs scored 14/28 and passed eight exact lookups. [Placement, quality and energy](inference-recipes/results/minimax-m31-flash-maya-xs-2026-11-20/)
- **Full GLM-5.3 on four Sparks.** The 753B model with a 6-bit output head delivered 32.85 generated tok/s for one client and 63.33 aggregate for four. Both it and the BF16 output head scored 21/28, with one item gained and one lost. [Recipe and measurements](inference-recipes/results/glm53-full-tp4-2026-11-19/)
- **Thinking Machines Inkling 2 Small on four Sparks.** The native 393.48 GB checkpoint delivered 39.34 generated tokens/s across two clients and 2.98 GPU J/output token on the switchless ring. Six inventory lookups were exact up to 3,597 input tokens. [Reproduce the run](inference-recipes/results/inkling2-gb10-runtime-2026-11-19/)
- **Qwen3.8 Flash-Next on one or two Sparks.** The one-Spark NVIDIA FP4-Local checkpoint reached 33.98 tok/s for one client and 72.86 aggregate for four, using 1.08 and 0.52 GPU J/token. The tuned two-Spark recipe reached 21.73 and 64.96 tok/s and returned the first delta sooner at one client. [Recipes, accuracy and raw timing](inference-recipes/results/qwen38-flash-next-2026-11-19/)
- **Selective BF16 with Qwen3.8 27B.** A mixed-first policy reproduced all 112 held-out BF16 answer letters while escalating 42 questions. It used 21.4% less two-Spark GPU energy than always querying BF16. Mixed alone was fastest and scored 73/112 against 72/112 for BF16 and the policy. [Live replay, accuracy and cost](inference-recipes/results/qwen38-27b-precision-routing-2026-11-19/)
- **Code editing with Qwen3.8 27B.** On one Spark, current-request suffix drafting reached 50.74 tok/s against 12.07 for ordinary decoding, with all 12 single-client edits matching token for token. Four clients reached 151.76 aggregate tok/s. Unrelated prose showed much smaller gains or a loss, depending on the proposer. [Four draft policies, paired outputs and GPU energy](inference-recipes/results/qwen38-27b-drafting-2026-11-19/)
- **Qwen3.8 27B on one Spark.** Mixed FP4 delivered 2.78–2.79× the BF16 throughput on the same machines at one client. BF16 scored 83/112 on a short-answer diagnostic and mixed FP4 scored 74/112 and 78/112 on the two machines. [Timing, paired answers and recipes](inference-recipes/results/qwen38-27b-precision-2026-11-19/)
- **One, two or four Sparks for Qwen3.8 27B.** One client reached 12.40, 20.46 and 33.94 output tok/s on one, two and four Sparks. Four clients reached 110.77 tok/s on the switchless RoCE ring using stock NCCL. With native BF16 those four delivered 54.33 tok/s and retained every sampled correctness verdict from the single-Spark BF16 baseline. [Transport, quality and GPU energy](inference-recipes/results/qwen38-27b-scaling-2026-11-19/) At 32 clients with 9 GiB KV per GPU, TP2 reached 354.39 tok/s at 0.21 GPU J/token. Two replicas reached 296.47 tok/s with a shorter p95 first-token wait (455 vs 587 ms). On the second pair, 3.25 GiB per GPU was enough for all 32 short requests and 351.58 tok/s on TP2. [Replica comparison](inference-recipes/results/qwen38-27b-pair-serving-2026-11-20/)
- **Nanbeige4.3-33B precision on one Spark.** At four clients, official BF16, official FP8 and community NVFP4 delivered 14.10, 24.14 and 43.50 aggregate tok/s. Free-answer scores were 63, 63 and 60 out of 112. GPU energy fell from 2.54 J/token with BF16 to 0.80 with NVFP4. [Three precision recipes and paired answers](inference-recipes/results/nanbeige43-33b-2026-11-19/)
- **GLM‑5.3‑Flash on two Sparks.** Two 8K-chunk runs improved 32K-input throughput by 10.8–11.2% over a 4K chunk. The two chunk settings scored 22/28 and 23/28 respectively on a short-answer check. A separate TensorFold kernel experiment gained 4.3% on the same-length workload while matching the control's sampled answers and likelihoods. [Chunk-size experiment](inference-recipes/results/glm53-prefill-2026-11-18/) · [Kernel patch and quality checks](inference-recipes/results/glm53-tensorfold-wide-2026-11-18/) On one GB10, refitting the existing FP4 codes and scales also reduced native matrix output error on 18 expert panels. [BF16 reference and numerical results](inference-recipes/results/glm53-flash-nvfp4-scales-2026-11-20/)
- **Qwen3 0.6B on four replicas.** Sixteen concurrent clients reached 1,894 aggregate output tok/s across four Sparks. At four clients a single Spark was faster. [Replication crossover](inference-recipes/results/qwen3-four-replicas-2026-11-19/)
